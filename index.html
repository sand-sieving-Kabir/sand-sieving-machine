Project Overview & Background

The Problem Statement

Traditional manual sand sieving using handheld wire mesh screens is extremely labor-intensive, slow, and induces severe physical fatigue (such as lower back strain and muscular stress) among construction workers[cite: 2]. Furthermore, it fails to meet large-scale project demands.

The Engineering Solution

This project presents a motorized mechanical screening device fully designed via SolidWorks[cite: 1]. Utilizing an electric motor, V-belt drive transmission[cite: 2], and an inclined rotating drum sieve, it efficiently separates fine aggregates from coarse debris continuously.

Motor Selection & Drive Calculations

Power & Torque Rating

The electric motor is chosen based on the estimated torque required to rotate a fully loaded sieve drum ($D = 600\text{ mm}$)[cite: 1] overcoming aggregate friction and rotational inertia.

Velocity Ratio & Speed Reduction

Utilizing a V-belt and pulley system[cite: 2], high motor RPM is efficiently stepped down to an optimal low drum rotation speed, ensuring smooth continuous separation without aggregate clogging.

Operational Efficiency

Ensures high mechanical reliability, stable power transmission under continuous load, and low energy consumption suited for site work.

Working Principle & Methodology

Power Transmission Flow

The electric motor delivers rotational output to the drive pulley. Through a reliable V-belt mechanism, motion is smoothly transferred to the driven assembly[cite: 2], inducing stable rotational and oscillatory movement across the sieve drum.

Screening & Separation

  • Feed Phase: Raw sand is loaded into the rotating mesh drum[cite: 1, 2].
  • Sieving: Fines fall through the perforations while stones move forward.
  • Discharge: Filtered sand slides cleanly via the sheet-metal chute into collection bins[cite: 2].
